## 1. The change

**Readers and writers of `ext_m` / `ext_q` / `bp_r` on the base** (all in `common/src/cubecl_join.rs`):

| where | role | words |
|---|---|---|
| `bucket_kernel` (one cube per (class, slot), thread i over the class's entries, coalesced) | reads `bp_r[i]` (and `bp_m`), writes entry `idx = slot·nbp + i` | `ext_m[2idx..]` (mask lo, hi), `ext_q[2idx..]` (the prefilter's two terms: carries and output digits at key level, else `a2 | a3 << 16`, `p2 | p3 << 16`) |
| `join_kernel`, wave load (per entry slot j of a wave: `e = lists[lbase + sl]`) | reads entry `gi = slot·nbp + e` and its residue | `bp_r[e]`, `ext_m[2gi], ext_m[2gi + 1]`, `ext_q[2gi], ext_q[2gi + 1]` |
| `JoinDevice` | `ext_m` (`max_slots · nbp · 8` bytes), `ext_q` (`max_slots · max(nbp, 1) · 8`), `bp_r` (the field's table, `nbp · 4`) | |
| `join_plan.rs` `Footprint` | `ext_m: nbp · 8`, `ext_q: max(nbp, 1) · 8` per slot; both in `per_slot` and `largest_per_slot` | |

Nothing else reads them: the check kernel, `flush_survivors` and the tests do not; the CPU join keeps its own host
structs. Everything after the wave's loads (the key-level arithmetic forming `ac`/`pc`, the AND, the walk, the
stage) is unchanged in every variant.

**Two layouts** (brief step 1), built and screened side by side:

- **E: one 32-byte record per (slot, entry)** (`EXT_REC_WORDS` = 8): mask lo, hi; the two prefilter words; r0; three
  zero words (written, so every sector the bucket kernel writes is whole). The join reads words 0–4 off one base and
  no longer binds `bp_r`. Footprint `ext_rec: max(nbp, 1) · 32` per slot: **16 more bytes per entry and slot**
  (b62 1e17: the ext data per launch 9.9 → 19.8 MiB).
- **F: one 16-byte record per (slot, entry)** (`EXT_REC_WORDS` = 4): mask lo, hi; the two prefilter words; r0 stays in
  `bp_r`. The join reads the record's four words off one base and `bp_r[e]`. Footprint `ext_rec: max(nbp, 1) · 16`:
  the two arrays' bytes, so the plans cannot move.

r0 does not fit beside the 16 bytes: it is below b^f0 (18 bits at b62–64, f0 = 3), and the record's free bits are
10 in the second prefilter word at key level and 64 − b in the mask (0 at b64). E pays for it with a second
16 bytes per entry.

In SASS (b62) both variants keep the join's 26 `LDG.E.CONSTANT`: per entry slot the base issues the list load, `bp_r`
and two pairs off two bases; E the list load and five off one base; F the list load, `bp_r` and four off one base.
The bucket kernel's stores: base 7 `STG`, E 11 (the record's eight words), F 7.

## 2. What the layouts change: sectors per entry (tree `X`, `cubecl_cuda_entry_sectors`, measured)

The brief asked to measure what matters, the sectors per entry. Test `cubecl_cuda_entry_sectors` (branch
`exp/join-entry-sectors`, not for main) launches five batches of a production plan's first slice (the first three,
the middle and the last batch of live partitions), reads every (slot, bucket) list back, and for each warp load of the
join's wave (32 consecutive list positions, counted once per batch of 256 of the bucket's tops) counts the distinct
32-byte sectors each layout would touch. Run on the base binary, so the lists are the production ones
(`sectors.log`, `raw/sectors.txt`):

| field | slots / nbp | warp loads (lanes each) | entries listed per key / class length | ascending neighbours | class entries spanned per lane | base: `bp_r` + `ext_m` + `ext_q` | E: 32-byte record | F: 16-byte record + `bp_r` | `lists` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| b62 1e17 | 6 / 108,355 | 5.54·10^6 (30.8) | 0.228 | 66 % | 36.3 | **2.524** (0.766 + 0.879 + 0.879) | **1.000** | **1.721** (0.955 + 0.766) | 0.152 |
| b62 1e16 | 9 / 94,818 | 4.31·10^6 (30.5) | 0.201 | 67 % | 33.6 | **2.531** | **1.000** | **1.724** | 0.150 |
| b60 1e16 | 6 / 101,400 | 7.21·10^6 (30.9) | 0.245 | 67 % | 32.7 | **2.497** | **1.000** | **1.706** | 0.150 |
| b64 1e16 | 10 / 93,846 | 13.7·10^6 (30.3) | 0.189 | 67 % | 31.2 | **2.543** | **1.000** | **1.731** | 0.148 |

- **The gather is as scattered as the brief assumed:** a bucket's list holds about a fifth of its class's entries,
  and a warp's 32 lanes read entries spread over ~1,000–1,150 consecutive class entries (32–36 per lane), a third of
  neighbours out of order. (How it arises: each of the bucket kernel's eight warps appends only a handful of entries
  to a key's list per pass over the class, and the warps' passes interleave.) Almost every lane
  gets its own sector in each of the three arrays: **2.5 sectors per entry on the base** (`bp_r` 0.77: 4-byte entries,
  eight to a sector, still mostly one lane each).
- **E cuts that to 1.0 sector per entry (−60 %), F to 1.72 (−32 %).** (The sector test is the measurement; the
  per-batch counts weight each bucket by its top batches, and five batches per field.)
- The data behind them: the ext arrays are 9.3–14.3 MiB per launch on the base and on F (18.6–28.6 MiB on E) in
  these first slices' layouts, against the RTX 3080's 5 MiB L2; `bp_r` is 366–423 KiB per field.
- At the measured ~3.4·10^7 entry loads per b62 1e17 batch (× 3785 batches ≈ 1.3·10^11 per field, if these batches
  are typical), F removes ~1.0·10^11 and E ~2.0·10^11 sector requests per 87 s field (3.3 and 6.3 TB): 38 and 72
  GB/s, 5 % and 9 % of the card's 760 GB/s DRAM bandwidth even if every one of them went to DRAM. §3 and §4 find
  that removing them gains nothing.

## 7. Why the bar is not met

- **Fewer sectors, no gain (measured).** The base reads 2.5 sectors per listed entry; F reads 1.72 at the same bytes
  and gains ×0.997–×1.006; E reads 1.0 and loses 0.1–1.8 % (screen). If the wave's entry gather were bound by its
  sectors, F would have saved a third of it and E three fifths. So in the full kernel the gather is not bound by its
  sector count.
- **The tops' gather was a different problem (measured sizes).** #241's record replaced three random reads from
  arrays of ~250 MB each per layout (b60 1e16: 5.3·10^6 top prefixes × 6 slots × 8 bytes; the record is ~1 GB)
  that the 5 MiB L2 cannot hold. The entries' arrays are 9.3–14.3 MiB per launch (`ext_m` + `ext_q`, from the
  measured nbp and slots) and `bp_r` is 0.37–0.42 MiB per field, so the L2 can hold `bp_r` and a sizeable part of
  the rest, and part of the base's 2.5 sectors likely never reach DRAM (inference from the sizes).
- **What L37's 10.5 s priced (inference; no profiler counters: the container lacks `CAP_SYS_ADMIN` and the driver
  restricts them to admins).** L37's figure was the difference between two stripped kernels (mode 9, no AND and no
  walk; mode 10, also no entry loads), in which every warp waits for its wave with nothing else to issue: the list
  load, the dependent entry loads at the listed index, and the key-level arithmetic. No layout shortens that chain;
  the record is still read at an index read from `lists`. In the full kernel five cubes share an SM and the others'
  AND and walk (54 % of the join, issue-bound) cover a wave's latency. L38 saw the same with the tops: its cycles
  gain was under half of what the ablation priced.
- **E's loss:** it writes 32 bytes per entry and slot (11 stores against 7 in the bucket kernel) and doubles the data
  the join reads (19.8 MiB per launch at b62 1e17 against 9.9), for records of which 12 bytes are padding; with the
  sectors not the limit, the extra bytes are what is left (0.1–1.8 %; inference).
- **Against the brief's expectation (4–6 %):** the expectation scaled the tops' result by the ablation's halves (10.5
  against 18.6 s); measured, the entry side gives 0 ± 1 %. Nothing in the layout of the entry loads is worth
  pursuing further on this evidence; the remaining wave cost, if any, is the dependent list → entry latency, and
  the average bucket (~340 entries at b62 1e17, L37) is a single wave of 512, so there is no next wave to prefetch.
