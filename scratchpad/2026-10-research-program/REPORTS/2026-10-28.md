# Research report, 2026-10-28 (day 1)

## What moved

- **The per-prefix cost puzzle (BACKLOG 68) is solved.** Join cost per top-layer prefix
  by base follows the number of roots of x² + x³ ≡ b(b−1)/2 (mod b−1): b58 has 4, b60 and
  b62 have 2, b64 has 8. Each top probes one bottom bucket per root, so b64 does 4× the
  ANDs of b62 and gets 4× the survivors. CPU counts on ivory and a GPU phase profile on a
  Vast RTX 3080 agree with each other and with your 9070 XT rates.
- **A sound lever fell out of the counts: the field-certificate bottom prune.** Every top's
  certificate contains the field's own certificate (the leading digits of n² and n³ fixed
  over the whole field). A bottom that shares a digit with the field certificate fails every
  AND in the field, so it can be dropped when the bottom list is built. On production-size
  1e14 fields that removes 73–86 % of bottoms at b58–b64. It is implemented on the fork
  (`rp/field-cert-prune`), exact against the reference join on the CPU, and the GPU A/B
  is in the section below.
- **Detailed mode has one clear lever on both CPU and GPU.** Both are compute-bound on
  per-number long division to extract ~50 digits. Finite differences (keep n², n³ and their
  difference terms as base-b digit arrays updated by addition) remove every division. The
  op-count estimate is 3–8× on CPU single-thread; the GPU kernel does the same divisions.
  A CPU prototype is next; the GPU port is the prize (fleet detailed runs on GPUs).
- **Two leads closed with numbers:** post-join filters (the final check is 3–11 % of time
  everywhere) and the interior-digit theorems (Drmota's extension gives joint uniformity of
  fixed interior positions with a non-effective error term; nothing computable at b58–64).
