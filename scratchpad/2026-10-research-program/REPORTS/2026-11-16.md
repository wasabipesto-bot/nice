### 1. Below exhaustive: a negative, with measurements behind each angle

The question: can any method do work that grows more slowly than the number of candidates?

| angle | what was tried | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| progressions and lattices | the middle digits as digits of polynomial progressions; solve-for-n methods; a search for any middle digit that partial information predicts | no: every middle digit behaves as a random function of every digit of n (bases 20–62); any choose-outputs-then-solve method loses to the tree by a counting argument |
| the exact cover | conditions from "the middle must be exactly the unused digits"; counting by inclusion–exclusion; SAT and CP solvers; representation techniques | no: such conditions rejected 0 of 4,400 survivors; solvers are no better than the best digit tree, which is ×4–6 worse than the join |
| measurement and adversary | every candidate of bases 10–33 (about 7 billion) tested for any cheap predictor of niceness; a lower-bound model | no hidden structure; given the end digits the middle is uniform to 1e-4 |

- **A lower-bound model now has its optimum computed exactly** (bases 20–33). In it the only
  slack is the pairing step: an ideal join that pays only for pairs that pass would sit on the
  floor, and today's join is estimated ×6–10 above that at bases 62 and 64.
- One new curiosity: when n is divisible by a power of a prime factor of the base, a fixed
  digit certifies more than two output digits. It breaks a rule I had stated, and is useless
  for finding anything.

### 2. A second-generation join: about ×5 at base 64 on paper, GPU number not yet measured

- **The design** takes part of that pairing slack. It is sound: an independent implementation
  written from the definition reproduces its survivor sets, including on real base-62 and
  base-64 units.
- **The estimate after adversarial review is about ×5 per base at base 64** (band ×2.6–9.6),
  not the designer's ×10. It matters for base 64 only, which is about 1.5 years at today's
  speed.
- **It needs a different server work unit** (a slice of the shared middle digits across the
  whole base), and every backend would need an implementation before a base could switch.
- **The GPU benchmark that decides it is staged, not run** (the harness refused the engineer's
  login to the rented card; I did not work around it). Stop numbers are fixed in advance:
  under ×5 I stop; ×6 or better is worth a run on your card.
