# Research report, 2026-11-06 (day 10; first full day on heavy+)

## What moved

- **Two new PRs, both independently reviewed before opening (the new rule), both exact:**
  - **PR #246 "Overlap join: a division-free middle-digit prefilter"** (stacked on #241). The
    per-pair prefilter now reads each pair's digits off a fixed-point fraction with
    multiplications instead of mod and div, tests membership with one funnel shift per digit on
    bit-reversed masks, gates the top mask by its seed flag and folds the residue test. The round
    before the stage shrinks from 71 to 41 instructions. ×1.086 at b62 1e17 (×1.103 clock-
    normalised), ×1.122 at b60 1e16, ×1.070 at b64; NiceMark ×1.061 over six rounds with every
    scenario up (b58 MSD-strong ×1.041). Exactness rests on an exhaustive host test over all
    6.6e11 operand triples at every base, a predicate-equivalence test for the membership form,
    and the usual device list. The reviewer re-derived the fixed-point bound (margin ≥ 161× at the
    worst base) and found no defect; its fixes were body text and one stale comment. One caveat,
    stated in the body: about half of the compiles of the level-4 kernel spill 28–44 bytes
    (ptxas aims for an occupancy the dynamic shared array hides); the spilled values are per
    batch, never in the walk, and the A/B rounds agree to 0.6%.
  - **PR #247 "CPU overlap join: step the top certificates through a key's prefixes"** (on main).
    The CPU join spends 42% of a production-shaped partition in `Base::cert`, mostly on two
    hardware divides per digit. Consecutive prefixes of a key differ by b³, so the finite-
    difference stepping from #240 applies within a partition, which is also why NiceMark sees it
    on the CPU. ×1.25–1.29 at b60 and b62 production shapes at 1 and 4 threads, ×1.04–1.09 at b64,
    NiceMark CPU nice-only ×1.186. The reviewer found no defect, confirmed the floating-point carry
    screen is sound with a wide margin, and asked for one missing carry test, now added and shown
    to catch two mutations the suite had missed. Measured on a Zen 2 VM; Zen 3/4 divide faster, so
    expect less there.
- **Per-wave base trims measured and parked:** the best pair is ×1.017 at b62 1e17 (×1.059 at
  b64), exact; two of the four trims lose, confirming the AND is bound by shared loads and the
  entry loads by latency.
