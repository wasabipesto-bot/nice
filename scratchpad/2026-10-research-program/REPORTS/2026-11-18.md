## Addendum, 08:15 UTC: the near-miss idea is dead, and I was wrong this morning

**Killed by its own stop line.** Counting repeats in the last stage would find about 5 or 6 numbers with all
but two digits over the rest of base 62 plus all of base 64. The bar was 30.

**My statement this morning was wrong.** I wrote that about 0.4 numbers with 61 of 62 digits should exist in
base 62 and that "today's search discards them". They exist, but not where the search looks:

- The search only visits n whose n² + n³ has the right remainder mod b − 1 (the residue filter). That remainder
  is the digit sum.
- A number with one digit doubled and one missing has the right digit sum only if the two digits are 0 and
  b − 1. Among the candidates the search visits, such numbers are exactly as rare as nice numbers.
- With two digits off, 2 of 61 classes (base 62) or 8 of 63 (base 64) remain.
- Finding the rest would mean searching every class: ×30 the work at base 62, ×8 at base 64.

The residue filter has been in the program's notes since its first day; I should have checked the idea against
it before starting an agent. The study itself is sound (prototype equal to brute force in 20 cases, cost
measured: +6 % on the CPU, about +1 % on the GPU by estimate) and is filed; nothing is proposed.

**One more thing I checked, in ten minutes and by argument only: could a second nice number be built instead of
found?**

- By the usual heuristic the expected count per base is lowest near base 55 and then grows without bound. All
  bases to 60 together carried 0.033 (and hold only 69); bases 62, 64 and 65 add 0.0007; the running total
  passes 1 at about base 125 to 128; base 200 should hold millions.
- So a second nice number almost surely exists, about sixty bases beyond what any enumeration reaches.
- I see no way to construct one: the square can be given designed digits (near-repdigits make them an
  arithmetic progression), but the cube of the same n then has digits that follow a quadratic law and cannot be
  the complement. Details in `CX/NOTES.md`. This is an argument, not a measurement.

**Where that leaves the research.** Shortcuts below exhaustive search, better pairing, by-products and
construction are now all closed with reasons. The one large lever I have found is the join redesign, and it
waits for decision 9. I have no other deep line today that needs neither a GPU nor you; I would rather say that
than start something small and call it research.
