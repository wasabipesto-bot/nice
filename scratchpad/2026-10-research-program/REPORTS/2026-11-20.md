## New research today

**How far the middle-out join's pairing is from its floor: measured, and closed.** The pairing kernel is 60 % of
a slice on the RX 9070 XT. A note (`CEIL/NOTES.md`) argues that generic indexing cannot beat it (it is an
orthogonal-vectors problem; a job has only 7 bits of chunk key), shows two restructurings that fail on paper,
and proposes one unused idea: sort a job's children by the size of their low four digits, so that the next
digits of n² and n³ below a top's certificate are constant over a chunk and the top can discard chunks from its
own digits. Exact counts on 10 base-64 and 2 base-65 slices (`CEIL/RESULTS.md`; the instrument reproduces the
kernel's counts and the card's 60.5 % busy lanes):

| variant | work against today's order and skip |
|---|---|
| longer sort key (10–16 bits), lexicographic order | ×1.00, ×1.0045 |
| children sorted by magnitude (the new idea) | ×0.89 (kills 50.6 % of visits, but empties the common masks) |
| best hybrid of the two | ×0.92 |
| sharing ORs between neighbouring probes | ×1.13–1.17 |
| a perfect skip test at any price (bound) | ×1.99 |
| base 65, best combination before its tables are paid for | ×1.35 (net: no gain, judged) |

Best exact variant ×1.18, under the ×1.3 set in advance: **the order-and-skip family is finished.** 1.1 billion
pairs the new tests would discard were checked by brute force: none nice. One lever is left and is not a count:
wider words per lane (128 or 256 children), ×1.0 to ×2 depending on what limits a slot on the card.
