## What moved

- **PR #235 "Overlap join: certify tops from one endpoint's digits and a carry test"** is open
  and green. Bit-identical certificates (102,365 intervals checked against the old scan and the
  CPU); ×1.075 at b62 1e17, ×1.089–1.096 at b62 1e16, ×1.053 at b60; NiceMark ×1.071 with every
  scenario faster. A CubeCL SPIR-V compiler stack overflow on the first version was traced (one
  frame per two code blocks on a 2 MiB thread) and fixed by branching once per chunk, which also
  left the top kernel smaller than main's.
- **Survivor handling is the next big one, and a prototype already clears the bar three times
  over:** running the prefilter inside the walk, before staging, cut a b62 1e17 field by 16.7%
  (225.7 → 188.0 s), exact to the last digit, and removed the stage overrun. The PR version is better still (PR #236): ×1.215 at b62 1e17, ×1.24 at b60, ×1.30 at b64,
  NiceMark ×1.194, join kernel registers 62 → 39; exact at every size, both lavapipe compilers pass.
- **The b62 1e17 profile** (the live field size) confirmed where time goes: top certification 38%,
  survivor handling 26%; 1e17 adds no cost term; batches and slices are measured non-levers.
- **Data-center cards are not worth renting:** on the join the L40S is the fastest card measured
  but 0.6–0.8× a 3080 per dollar; the H100 about 0.1×. Break-even prices: L40S ≈ $0.35/h, H100 ≈
  $0.30/h.
- **The probability model's tail holds to three missing digits** (within 2–3%), gains a
  leading-digit correction, and at two missing digits runs 12% under the data with wide error
  bars: today's 5.45% is best read as about 4.7% (3.7–5.3%). Costs and search order unchanged.
- The two-word mask for the native kernel is implemented and exact but held: its gain is only
  predicted on Apple, and forcing it on CUDA cost 24%.
- **Two more levers were measured on the combined tree and both land just under the bar,
  exact:** a cheaper top certificate (endpoint differences and a bit-length digit count) gives
  ×1.045 at b62 1e17, ×1.059 at b60, NiceMark ×1.041, and shrinks the kernel by about a quarter;
  a bucket-sized tail wave removes 90% of the join's empty lanes for ×1.040 at b62 1e17, ×1.028
  at b60, NiceMark ×1.078, at the cost of one register and unchecked behaviour under wgpu and
  Metal. Both are parked on experiment branches with full numbers; your call whether a ×1.04–1.06
  exact change is worth a PR.
- **Last scoping of the day, running: an early reject in top certification.** 68% of certified
  pairs fail, yet each pays the whole digit conversion (55% of the top kernel); if failures show
  up in the first produced digits, stopping there is the next 5%.
