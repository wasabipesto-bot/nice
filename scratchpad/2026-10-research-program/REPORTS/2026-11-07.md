## What moved

- **Two PRs opened, one more PR-ready and held at the cap, all three independently reviewed,
  all exact:**
  - **PR #249 "Overlap join: a static bucket order for the top layer"** (on #246). The host sorts
    each slice's top layer once by the bucket group it feeds; a slot only rotates the class, so
    every slot sees the same contiguous groups. The top kernels append kept tops straight into
    per-(slot, group) segments, and the join reads each bucket's tops as contiguous records. Two
    kernels and five buffers are deleted. ×1.158 at b62 1e17, ×1.154 at b60, ×1.222 at b64,
    NiceMark ×1.119 over six rounds, every scenario up. It beat yesterday's measured ceiling because
    the FD kernel's per-top atomics and the join's gathers go too. A second commit checks the
    survivor stage once per top batch, an exact ×1.02–1.03 on top. The reviewer re-derived the
    bucket algebra, wrote its own host test against the CPU join's bucket rule (now in CI), and
    corrected one premise of mine: the FD kernel steps partitions per prefix, so the sort cannot
    touch its stepping.
  - **PR #248 "CPU overlap join: a fixed-point middle-digit prefilter"** (on #247). The CPU twin of
    #246: ×1.07–1.15 on production-shaped partitions, NiceMark CPU ×1.088, the exhaustive
    fixed-point test exact on the CPU too. The review caught a 48 → 64-byte growth of the top
    record for a field only tests read, now reverted with a compile-time size assertion.
  - **FD top kernel, round 2** (`pr/top-fd-round2`, on #246; held because five PRs are open):
    ×1.082 at b62 1e17, ×1.070 at b60, ×1.093 at b62 1e16, NiceMark ×1.029 with the lowest
    scenario at ×1.003. The stepped slot is 31% cheaper. A first form that also cut slices failed
    the NiceMark rule on b60 (×0.976: a wider slice prunes less at the field certificate), so
    slices stay as they were. Under review now; it opens as soon as something merges.
