# Research report, 2026-11-02 (day 6)

## What moved

- **Two new PRs, both from today's scoping, both exact, both on the live field sizes:**
  - **PR #240 "Overlap join: step the top certificates through a batch's partitions"** (stacked
    on #237, which is on #235). Consecutive partitions move a top prefix's interval by exactly
    one base power, so the next partition's square and cube digits are additions of shifted digit
    arrays, the same finite-difference step the detailed mode got in #221/#225. Long division
    happens only when a slot seeds (the first of a batch, a gap, a clipped block). Against #237
    alone: ×1.080 at b62 1e17, ×1.116 at b62 1e16, ×1.065 at b60 1e16, ×1.043 at b58 1e16; with
    #236 underneath it is ×1.143 at b62 1e17 and ×1.084 at b60. The kernel itself is ×1.5–1.6.
    Power-of-two bases keep the old kernel (b64 neutral). **NiceMark is neutral by construction**
    (see Verdicts). Big diff: +1495/−169 lines in one file; a new device test compares both kernels
    on 279 batches and 238 M tops.
  - **PR #239 "Overlap join: test entries against a transposed copy of the top batch"** (stacked on
    #236). A bottom mask has exactly 12 digits, so a chunk of 32 tops is tested with 12 ORs of
    per-digit top bitmaps instead of 32 two-word tests. Gated at batches of ≥ 192 tops. Against
    #236 alone: ×1.055 at b62 1e17, ×1.050 at b62 1e16, ×1.062 at b60, ×1.176 at b64, NiceMark
    ×1.075 with every scenario ≥ ×1.017. 1e14 fields lose 2–7% from the kernel's footprint
    (2 KiB more shared memory, 5 cubes per SM instead of 6), stated in the body; 1e14 is far below
    production sizes.
- **#235, #236 and #237 were rebased onto main** after #234/#238 touched the changelog (code
  byte-identical, each entry now carries its own PR link as #234's convention does); all green.
- **The join's AND loop is bound by shared-memory loads, not ALU:** the transposed form cut ALU
  work ~3× but shared wavefronts only ~25% (2-way bank conflicts on the digit gathers), so the AND
  got ~×1.3 cheaper, not the 2–4× I had estimated. That caps what any AND-side rewrite can give.
- **Profile after #237** (b62 1e17): top 35%, join 53%, check 6.5%; at b60 1e16 the join is 58%.

## Verdicts

- **NiceMark cannot see #240's gain.** It samples each field's partitions in bit-reversed order
  (`sample_order`), so no batch holds two consecutive partitions and every FD slot seeds by
  division; production runs partitions consecutively. I endorse #240 on the whole-field runs,
  which is the production path, and the body says so. Worth knowing when you re-pin references:
  a nice-only benchmark that samples consecutive partitions would track production better.
- **#239 is a modest, clean win** (×1.05–1.06 live, ×1.075 NiceMark) with a known small-field
  cost. If you would rather not carry the 1e14 regression, say so and I will close it; the
  alternative (choosing the kernel per field from the host's tops-per-bucket estimate) is a
  follow-up.
- L18 (bit-sliced bucket scan) is superseded by L34 in the other orientation; recorded.
- The CPU join already tests 8 masks per AVX-512 instruction, so no CPU analogue of #239.
