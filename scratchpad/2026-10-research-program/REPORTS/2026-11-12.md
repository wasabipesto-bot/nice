# Research report, 2026-11-12

## Correction to yesterday's addendum

Yesterday I told you that on the RX 9070 XT the join's fixed per-batch work is about a third of
a field and that survivor handling is nearly free there. **Both are wrong.** Those figures came
from runs with parts of the kernel switched off, and with less work that card drops to half its
clock and a third of its power (1.5–1.9 GHz at about 95 W, against 3.2 GHz at 300 W). The agent
compared those runs with full-speed ones; I passed its explanation on without opening the clock
samples it had recorded. Re-derived from the same logs, base 62 (base 60 is within two points):

| share of a field | 9070 XT, corrected (range) | yesterday's claim | RTX 3080 |
|---|---|---|---|
| fixed per-batch work | 7–13 % | 36 % | 11 % |
| digit-overlap test (AND) | 12–19 % | 13 % | 15 % |
| survivor handling: walk | 5–6 % | 3 % | 9 % |
| survivor handling: middle-digit prefilter | 15–28 % | 1 % | 16–20 % |
| full check | 17 % | 17 % | 11–12 % |
| top certificates | 22 % | 22 % | 23 % |

So the card's profile is NVIDIA's, with a dearer check and possibly a dearer prefilter. The
ranges are what the half-clock logs can bound (amended 10:00 UTC: this morning's version gave
single figures from the top of each range and called them good to a few points, which was too
tight). Only a run on the card at full load settles them. **What stands:** every A/B from the window (#250 ×1.04,
#251 ×1.13, #254 ×1.09 at base 64) and the two tunings; all of those ran at full clock.

## Addendum, 10:00 UTC: the harness is ready

- **CI is green on all four PRs** at their merge commits.
- **The harness** is on fork branch `exp/n16-join-base`. It measures a part's cost by running
  that part twice at full load (results unchanged, checked against the CPU join on CUDA and on
  lavapipe), logs clock and power beside every timing and flags any run off the card's operating
  point. One script runs the whole window: the must-haves are done by minute 45 and it stops
  itself at minute 84.
- **The RTX 3080 measured the same way** (share of a field at base 62 / base 64): prefilter
  16 / 21 %, AND 15 / 15 %, check 11 / 11 %, walk 9 / 13 %. Removing parts gives the same within
  3 points, and NVIDIA keeps its clock when work is removed, so the earlier NVIDIA profiles stand.
- **A concrete lead for your card, not yet measured there.** Compiling the join kernel offline
  with the AMD driver's compiler shows clean code but a large shared-memory footprint per work
  group (18.9 KiB), which limits how many groups the card runs at once. Halving the top batch
  frees a third of it, and that is the tuning that gained 4–5 % on your card yesterday for no
  known reason. Shrinking the survivor buffer frees as much, and on NVIDIA it costs nothing
  (measured +0.1 %), so it would need no per-device setting. Three rows of the window test it.
- **The denser batch plan for wgpu cards** (×1.04 on demoux yesterday) is a candidate branch,
  `exp/n14-wgpu-dense-plan`: discrete adapters with a 2 GiB budget only, so the M4 and integrated
  GPUs keep today's plan. wgpu reports no memory size, so the rule cannot tell a 4 GB discrete
  card from a 16 GB one. Not reviewed, no PR; the window measures it first.

The window request above stands and is now ready to use: 90 minutes at most, card to itself.
The plan is `N16/WINDOW-PLAN.md` in the program directory.
