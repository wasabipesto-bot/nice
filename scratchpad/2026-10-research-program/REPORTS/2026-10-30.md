## What moved

- **PR #221 is open: "Detailed mode: digit arrays by finite differences instead of
  division."** CPU detailed score ×6.76 on a 15-CPU Broadwell box (b40 ×5.6, b50 ×8.0,
  single-thread ×6.9), no regression at any thread count, exact on every test including
  whole-field results and near-miss lists. One commit, 556 lines, one CHANGELOG sentence.
- **#215 and #216 merged yesterday** (you did that at 12:56 UTC), so the join re-profile
  below is on the main that has the prune and the two-entry waves.
- **A join lead measured, then parked.** At 1e14 fields the per-field fixed cost is 40–74%
  of the time, and 64 partitions per launch instead of 16 (possible once the bottom buffers
  are sized by the pruned lists) is 1.14–1.26× faster there. But it is neutral at 1e16, and
  the live fields are 1e16 at b60 and 1e17 at b62 (checked on the data API), so it does not
  help production and NiceMark is flat. I had called 1e14 "production size" from a stale
  code comment; corrected. Branch kept for small-field work.
- **That work found a latent race in the join kernel, and its fix is PR #222.** The flush
  decision after each 32-top chunk reads the shared stage count without a barrier before the
  next chunk appends, so a late plane can flush alone and the cube's barriers pair up wrongly:
  stale list entries get fully checked, and entries can be overwritten so survivors are never
  checked. Rare in today's layouts, frequent with more partitions per launch, possible in the
  1e16 layouts by the code. One barrier fixes it at no measurable cost.
- **The GPU port of the finite-difference scheme works: 2.6× (b40) and 3.2× (b50) over the
  current detailed kernel on the production path, 3.2–4.0× once a buffer-readback waste it
  found is fixed on both sides.** Exact against the CPU on every window including the split
  one. The card was power-capped, so about 1.18× of that is clock; per clock it is 2.2–2.7×.
  Tomorrow: confirm on an uncapped card, then its PR, plus a small separate PR for the
  readback fix (+5–8% on its own).
