## 1. Status of the big claims (they do not bear on the search)

**Alpöge, RH/GRH with Claude (announced 2026-11-20).** The strongest verification evidence so far is a primary
page: Andrew Sutherland's record at math.mit.edu/~drew/grh (last update 2026-11-22 16:20 UTC; he says the checks
were run with Claude Code). Done: all 175,187 modules compile under Lean/Mathlib v4.33.1 with zero errors; the
five headline theorems depend only on `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`; an independent sandboxed
`lake build Main` reproduced the export byte for byte; `leanchecker`, the independent Rust kernel nanoda and
lean4lean accepted it; con-leche and con-ron (master builds) accepted the proof against the challenge
statements. Still running at that update: Lean's default and paranoid kernels on the export and the end-to-end
`lake comparator`. Caveats listed there: one `.olean` is nondeterministic (a Lean race, fixed in v4.36.0); five
modules needed a higher lean4lean step limit. Secondary reports (cryptoslate, unite.ai, DEV Community, casrai;
weak sources, repeating each other) say the Lean and Mathlib communities "certified" it on 11-21 and that the
Lean FRO and Mathlib Initiative issued a joint statement; Clay still lists the problem as open. What is
verified is therefore "the Lean statement is Mathlib's own `RiemannHypothesis` and the formal proof is
kernel-checked"; what is not: a human-readable proof (Alpöge says he will write one), and refereeing.

**Masullo, "The Music of the Primes" (940 pages, announced 2026-11-20 via Vakil).** Unverified and in trouble.
Reports (blogs, GitHub audit repos, some AI-assisted and not peer reviewed; weak) say several people found
problems; one audit repo (dvgyl/masullo-grh-proof-audit) concludes it does not prove GRH as written (main trace
identity Thm 12.5.1.1 fails with the printed Haar measure; the theorem that would put zeros on the critical line
has a placeholder statement). Vakil's post makes no claim of correctness and the author himself says the text is
unfinished. Treat as a draft with gaps, not a proof.

**OpenAI quasi-RH (preprint 2026-09-30, zero-free region Re s > 7/8) and Landau-Siegel exclusion (preprint
2026-10-01).** No journal acceptance found. Independent human write-ups exist: Banks arXiv 2610.15483 (explicit
exposition), Kerr arXiv 2610.21099 (pigeonhole replaces interpolation), 2610.30476 (elementary explicit proof),
Hexagon variants. Quanta (10-19, from the earlier sweep) said experts largely agree the quasi-RH result is
correct. Status: widely examined preprints, unrefereed, no reported refutation.

## 2. Hits that bear on the search

- **Digits of squares/cubes/polynomial values, pandigital powers, restricted digits:** no relevant new work.
  Only standing background came back (Wu 2403.20304; Drmota-Mauduit-Rivat; Spiegelhofer on cubes
  2308.09498; Maynard's digits of primes). Sixth consecutive null on this theme.
- **Orthogonal vectors:** no new algorithm in the window. Most recent items are all earlier: Dürr et al.
  arXiv 2507.11098 (v2 June 2026; 1.25^d n randomized, 1.16^d n with computer-aided evaluation), "New and
  Improved Concrete Lower Bounds for Orthogonal Vectors" arXiv 2607.23799 (July; unconditional, concrete
  models; abstract only, I did not read what the models are), "Online OV Revisited" 2605.04798. Nothing
  changes the CEIL verdict at d = 64.
- **Subset query / partial match / disjointness circuits:** arXiv 2610.10446 "Subset Sum via Partial Match"
  (Li, Ye; v1 2026-10-07, revised 11-02) beats the 2^(n/2) meet-in-the-middle barrier for subset sum by
  partial match; theory, no GPU, and the join there is on sums, not digit masks. Worth a skim for any
  partial-match trick on structured vectors; no bearing found at abstract level. 2603.15565 (depth-2 linear
  circuits for disjointness, Mar 2026) and 2509.14489 (O(N^1.2495) disjointness circuit) are the nearest
  disjointness-matrix results; asymptotic, not practical here.
- **GPU subset matching:** only Rogora et al. EuroSys 2017 (old). Nothing new.
- **Meet-in-the-middle with carries:** nothing.

## 3. AI-assisted formal proof

- **Lean v4.36.0 (released 2026-11-16; v4.37.0-rc1 on 11-18).** From the primary notes: `bv_decide` gains `+uf`
  (uninterpreted functions returning Bool/BitVec); `grind` bit-vector/fixed-width homomorphisms extended
  (masks, concat, rotations, shifts, signed comparisons, exponentiation, complement) and a fix for
  multiplying by a numeral near the modulus that exhausted `lia` steps; a new opt-in Sym-based normalizer
  (`backward.grind.normalizer false`) with an `arith` simproc that solves over `Fin n`, `UInt8`, `BitVec w`;
  simprocs for bitwise operations on `UIntX`/`IntX`; `Sym.Simp.evalGround` for `Int.toNat`, `Int.natAbs`, `Fin`;
  compiler constant folders for `Nat`/`UInt`; kernel fixes for proofs rejected from `grind`'s ring module; type
  class results persisted across commands (about 14 % faster stdlib build) and several quadratic elaboration
  slowdowns removed. No mention of `omega` or `decide`. Relevance: a proof about digit arithmetic and list
  algorithms is mostly `Nat` div/mod by base, `List` induction, `omega`; the `grind` arithmetic and `Nat`
  folding items are the ones to try on those side goals; `bv_decide` matters only if the model is bit-sliced
  `BitVec 64` masks (the join's word-level test), where `+uf` and the 64-bit homomorphisms help. Worth
  upgrading the toolchain only if the Lean model's Mathlib pin allows it; the Alpöge build used 4.33.1.
- **Agentic Lean tooling, as titles (weak; abstract level only):** "Lea: an agent backbone for
  mathematician-controlled formalization in Lean" (arXiv 2611.08849; authors note output needs refactoring
  and review before Mathlib quality); "Numina Fuse: interactive autoformalization in Lean 4" (2610.33860);
  "AIProver" (2610.05367); "LeanFlow, workflow-driven autoformalization" (2607.20503); "Sorries are not the
  hard part: an expert-review case study of a semi-autonomous formalization" (2606.13925); "A Rust-to-Lean
  verification pipeline with AI provers: an experience report" (2605.30106; Aeneas/Hax on independent Lean
  release tracks). A listed "Paper2Lean" (arXiv 2611.26272) has an identifier that would fall after today's
  date, so I do not trust that listing. The Mathlib Initiative's "Formal Frontier" project is named as a
  pipeline of interacting AI agents (weak, from a search synthesis).
- **Compiled-model conformance:** nothing authoritative. The Rust-to-Lean experience report (2605.30106)
  is the closest to "link a compiled implementation to a Lean model": it takes a pure functional Lean
  translation of Rust and reports friction from independent version tracks. A "lean-in-lean" GitHub repo
  claims a formal model of the Lean compiler pipeline (5,406 lines, 105 theorems); self-reported, unreviewed.
  Extraction from Lean to Rust/Go is described as open research; Lean to C is the supported path.
- **Net for a proof of a few thousand lines:** the main change this week is the demonstration, from the
  Alpöge check, that independent kernels (nanoda, lean4lean, leanchecker) and comparator tools exist and
  work at 175k-module scale; checking a few thousand lines with them is cheap. Axiom audit
  (`#print axioms`) plus a statement-comparison tool is the cheap trust package.

## 4. Platform notes (changes since 2026-11-15 only)

- **CubeCL:** no release after v0.12.0-pre.2 (09 Nov) on the releases page; no final 0.12.0. The pre.2 notes
  (read earlier) stand: #1970 eager store, #2105 fold by one for remainder/mul_hi. Standing advice (do not
  upgrade from 0.11.x; revisit at 0.12 final) unchanged. A downstream fork (tensor4all t4a-cubecl 0.12.0,
  PR #38) exists; ignore.
- **wgpu/naga (upstream gfx-rs):** a Dependabot PR shows wgpu v30.0.2 dated 2026-11-12 (a GLSL-in fix; one
  listed item, before the window start by three days, weak source). No subgroup item in the window found.
  Not to be confused with the Go port gogpu (naga v0.22.5/0.22.6, 11-17/11-19: subgroup scan and ballot IR
  work for DXIL/GLSL/MSL/HLSL), which is a different project.
- **Mesa:** 26.3.0 was released 2026-11-09 (before the window; the 11-15 sweep reported only 26.2.7). It
  includes compute fixes (compute userdata, GFX12 SH_REG for compute, ELF for ACO binaries) and fixes an
  ACO 26.2 regression where `s_bitset0` retargeted to exec dropped the VCC condition (RDNA2-RDNA4); adds
  GFX1171 "RDNA 4m" ids. **26.3.1 (2026-11-18, in window)**: ACO fixes for quad alignment of SGPRs in post-RA,
  split-vector reassignment with late-kill operands, and SSBO SMEM loads respecting barriers; RADV
  foreign-queue and conditional-rendering fixes. No RDNA4-specific compute bug, no integer-division item
  (closest: a NIR unsigned-range overflow fix for multiplication by constants). Worth the 9070 XT host
  picking up 26.3.1 only if the SMEM-barrier fix is relevant to an observed miscompare.
- **WebGPU/Vulkan subgroups:** nothing dated in the window. Standing facts: Chrome 145 shipped the WGSL
  `subgroup_uniformity` extension; the subgroup-matrix proposal says implementations do not give reliable
  reconvergence; `VK_KHR_shader_subgroup_uniform_control_flow` is the Vulkan guarantee. No change.
