## B. Targeted questions

### B1. Divergence/compaction techniques beyond ballot+prefix-sum, break-even points

One new candidate surfaced this week: **"GTaP: A GPU-Resident Fork-Join
Task-Parallel System with a Pragma-Based Interface"** ([2604.05982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05982),
Apr 2026, pre-window but relevant to the question itself so followed up).
It implements GPU-resident work stealing plus an optional
**"Divergence-Aware Queueing (DAQ)"** that partitions task queues by a
user-defined criterion specifically to cut warp divergence — this is a named,
real instance of the "per-warp work queues" family B1 asked about. Checked
via abstract only: it does **not** report a measured break-even point for
item cost vs. density — its benchmarks are against OpenMP/OpenCilk on FMM
traversal and k-clique counting, not a density sweep. Actionable as a paper
to read in full if the two-phase/DAQ family is worth prototyping, but not a
citable number for the break-even question.

Also re-surfaced (not new vs. sweep #2, but with one added data point): a
real production use of warp-aggregated atomics was found — an AMReX
(DOE exascale framework) GitHub PR applying warp-aggregated atomics to
`DenseBins` construction, plus the standing NVIDIA figure ("up to 32× fewer
atomics... filtering kernel up to 21× faster on Kepler"). Same technique
already on record; no new break-even-vs-density number for the ~1–3-items-
per-32-lane regime specifically. **No paper found this week quantifies the
break-even point directly** — this remains the same open gap sweep #1 left.

### B2. Fastmod/Barrett for 16–20-bit, 64-bit one-hot mask on 32-bit hardware, digit-distinctness test

- **New, useful confirmation (not a new paper, a clarified fact)**: Lemire's
  `fast_division` library documentation states its 32-bit-register variant
  natively supports **16-bit divisors** — i.e. the fastmod/reciprocal-multiply
  family's natural operating range is exactly the 16–20-bit dividend size
  this project needs on 32-bit GPU lanes, not just a CPU-sized technique
  scaled down. Still CPU-library-only, same caveat as sweep #2's Barrett
  finding (no GPU-specific paper found applying it at this width).
- **64-bit one-hot mask on 32-bit hardware**: no dedicated trick or paper
  found beyond the standard, already-obvious emulation (two 32-bit shifts
  plus a compare/select on which half the bit lands in — `lo = g<32 ? 1<<g :
  0; hi = g>=32 ? 1<<(g-32) : 0`). Nothing better was found this week.
- **Digit-distinctness-and-absence test**: no dedicated bit-trick found.
  "Bit Twiddling Hacks" (Stanford/Sean Eron Anderson) remains the closest
  generic reference; nothing specific to "two small values differ and are
  both absent from a 64-bit set" turned up.
- **Net for B2**: one incremental confirmation (fastmod's bit-width fit),
  otherwise a repeat of sweep #3's finding — the mask-build and
  distinctness-test questions look like genuine literature gaps, narrow
  enough to this project's exact predicate that further search time against
  them is likely wasted.

## C. Verdict

**Four consecutive sweep-weeks (10-29, 11-01, 11-03, 11-05) with zero
relevant new arXiv papers**, the last of them run with a deliberately
widened, 7-category, 4-week-window term list specifically to rule out "the
old terms were too narrow" as the explanation. They weren't too narrow:
direct listing scans of the most recent days in cs.DC and math.CO, a
first-50-of-854 scan of math.NT's whole month, and ten web-search sweeps
across every widened term bucket all came back empty or false-positive
(ML "compaction," warp-programming correctness/profiling tools that happen
to share vocabulary with divergence-reduction techniques but aren't them).

**Per the task's own pre-registered rule, drop the arXiv-canvass part
(Section A) of this weekly sweep going forward.** This project's actual
object — joint digit structure of n² and n³, exact survivor-walk/prefilter
kernel shapes — is not a live arXiv topic, and widening the terms once (as
specified) didn't change that conclusion.

**Keep, lighter-weight**: the OEIS/MathWorld check (confirmed this week:
nothing to monitor, could drop to an occasional spot-check rather than
weekly) and the targeted B-question sweep, since that's the part that has
actually produced usable results across this program (sweep #2's
cost/selectivity filter-ordering optimality paper and the standing
warp-aggregated-atomics reference; this week's GTaP/DAQ lead and the
fastmod bit-width confirmation). Recommend future weeks spend the budget on
B-style targeted questions plus a quick repo/OEIS spot-check, not a full
7-category arXiv canvass, unless the project opens a new sub-problem general
enough to plausibly have active literature.

**Tooling note for future runs**: `oeis.org` returned HTTP 403 to the
WebFetch tool on every endpoint tried (search page and a plain sequence
page) — OEIS appears to actively block this fetch path, not just rate-limit
it. Any future OEIS check needs a different access method.
