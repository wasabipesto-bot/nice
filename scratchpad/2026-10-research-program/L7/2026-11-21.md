## What has happened recently (as reported)

- **OpenAI's math release of 2026-10-06** (several hundred results from an unreleased model, many with Lean
  formalisations). The headline is a **quasi-Riemann hypothesis**: every nontrivial zero in a strip narrower than
  the critical strip (real part below 11/12 in the 49-page paper experts have read; a 7/8 version of 199 pages is
  the Lean-checked one; a follow-up paper says it covers every Dirichlet L-function). Quanta (10-19): experts
  "largely agree that the result is correct"; follow-up preprints refine the constant.
- In the same batch a claimed **exclusion of Landau–Siegel zeros**, with human follow-ups (Banks, Kerr, a sketch
  on Tao's blog); unrefereed.
- AI systems on Erdős problems through 2026 (about a hundred moved to "solved", most by literature
  identification; a counterexample to the unit-distance conjecture in May); the prime-gap record lowered by a
  human and then by AI in the summer.
- Orthogonal vectors: Dürr, Kipouridis, Lampis, Węgrzycki (ICALP 2026): about 1.25^d · n randomized, from
  Williams's 1.35^d · n; still no n^(2−ε). Average-case OV (Alman, Andoni, Zhang, 2024): n^(2−Ω(log log c / log c))
  at dimension c·log n, by batch polynomial evaluation, asymptotic, finds a pair rather than listing all.
- Digits of squares, cubes, polynomial values; pandigital powers: nothing new in 2026 found (the standing
  references are Wu 2024 on pandigital squares and the Drmota–Mauduit–Rivat line).

## Does any of it touch the project? [my judgement]

1. **Quasi-RH / GRH / Siegel zeros: no.** They control primes in progressions and short character sums. The
   search uses no primes and no characters; the digit model rests on exponential sums over digits; the event
   "all b digits distinct" has probability about e^(−b), far below what any equidistribution theorem reaches.
2. **The residue filter gives nothing beyond b ≡ 3 mod 4** [P, and checked for b ≤ 3000]: for even b the target
   residue is 0 (r = 0 always passes); for odd b with m = b − 1 = 2^a·u one needs v2(r²(r + 1)) = a − 1, which an
   odd r gives whenever a ≥ 2 and nothing gives when a = 1. So that family is the whole of what this argument
   can exclude (Wu's 2024 theorem for pandigital squares is the same argument).
3. **Orthogonal vectors: confirms yesterday's verdict (CEIL.1).** At d = 64 and lists of 472 × 8,400,
   1.25^64 ≈ 1.6e6 per vector is far above the 8,400 a flat scan reads; the average-case result is for random
   vectors at d = c·log n and gains n^(o(1)) at c ≈ 5. A filter family "top ⊆ F ⊆ complement of child" would test
   no dead pair at all but needs about 1e11 filters to cover (C(27, 13)/C(64, 30) ≈ 1e-11 per filter): no.
4. **What the wave does change is the price of verification.** Lean-checked proofs of this size are now
   produced routinely; a machine-checked exactness proof of the middle-out join (coordinate bijection, both
   certificates, coverage) is a smaller object than those. The existing Lean work is a draft PR the user has
   parked; noted, not pursued.
