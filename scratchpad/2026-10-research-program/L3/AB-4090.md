# L3 second card: readback PR and FD PR on an RTX 4090 (2026-10-30)

**Result.** On a 4090 the FD PR is **3.46× at b40 and 3.92× at b50** over main on the NiceMark detailed
GPU windows (NiceMark 11,493 → 42,296, 3.68×). On 1e10-number production fields it is **3.06× and 3.77×**.
The readback PR alone gives **+14 % and +9 %** on the NiceMark windows, +1–2 % on 2e9 windows, and
nothing measurable on 1e10 fields. Every result is identical across the three trees, and every FD
exactness test passes on this card.

**Trees.** Each was built on the box with the production profile (`cargo build --release -p nice_client
--features gpu`, LTO, one codegen unit), each in its own target dir:

- **main** = upstream `46e4dc4`;
- **rb** = `pr/gpu-detailed-readback` `6a1e396`;
- **fd** = `pr/gpu-detailed-fd` `f3a33cf`, stacked on rb.

The only change to any worktree is an untracked `common/examples/l3win.rs` (section 3). Raw logs are in
`pr-raw/4090/`.

## 1. Card, power and clocks

- **Box.** Vast 58401579 `nice-research-fd-4090`:
  - RTX 4090, VBIOS 95.02.47.00.01, driver 570.195.03, CUDA 12.8;
  - AMD EPYC 9354P host with a cgroup quota of 10.24 CPUs;
  - PCIe 4.0 ×16 (gen 4 under load).

  Sources: `box_info.txt`, `nvsmi_end.txt`.
- **Power: not capped.** The enforced limit is **480 W** (default 450 W, max 600 W). Under the NiceMark
  load:
  - main and rb draw about 370–400 W (peaks 450–470 W on the short windows);
  - fd draws about 325 W.

  `SW Power Cap` was **Not Active** in all 24 mid-run snapshots (`ab_*/nm/q_*.txt`).
- **Thermal: limited.** `SW Thermal Slowdown` was **Active** in every snapshot:
  - GPU at 85–90 °C against an 83 °C target, with the fan at 100 %;
  - so clocks sit well below the 3,105 MHz maximum.

  This card is thermally limited, where the 3080 was power-limited.
- **SM clock under load.** Medians from `nvidia-smi dmon`, rounds 4–6:

| tree | b40_detailed | b50_detailed | power |
|---|---|---|---|
| main | 2,550–2,565 MHz | 2,500–2,520 MHz | 370–380 W |
| rb | 2,475–2,565 MHz | 2,415–2,460 MHz | 370–405 W |
| fd | 2,625 MHz | 2,625 MHz | 320–328 W |

- **Why rb clocks lower.** It keeps the GPU busier than main, so it heats more and the thermal controller
  drops it about 90 MHz.
- **FD's clock advantage is small.** FD runs cooler, which buys it **1.03–1.05×** clock over main and
  1.06–1.09× over rb. On the 3080 the power cap was worth 1.18×.
- **Backend.** `auto` picked **CubeCL CUDA** on every tree, in every run: `GPU initialized: CubeCL CUDA
  device 0, batch size 50000000`.

### Spreads

- **Rounds 4–6 were clean.** Within-tree spread was 0.2–0.4 % for main and fd and 1.2–3.7 % for rb. Their
  ratios equal the 6-round ones within 0.5 %: rb/main 1.138 / 1.101 / 1.119, fd/main 3.460 / 3.915 / 3.679.
- **Rounds 2–3 were disturbed.**
  - In round 2, all three trees ran at about 305 W and 2.24–2.55 GHz at the same 89–90 °C. That is a
    hotter environment, plausibly a neighbouring card.
  - Main's round 3 (italic in the table) ran **18 % below its own rate per clock**: 8.08e9 per GHz at b40,
    against 9.81–9.95e9 in its other five rounds. rb and fd, which ran either side of it,
    stayed within 1 % of their per-clock medians.
  - Only main copies 20 MB back per call, so a host-side PCIe or memory disturbance would hit main alone.
    That is a hypothesis; I did not test it.
- **Medians are robust.** These rounds widen the 6-round ranges (main 16–20 %) but not the medians.

### Per clock

- rb/main per clock is 1.18× and 1.13×, more than its raw gain, because rb runs about 3.5 % slower clocks.

## 3. Fixed 2e9-number windows

The 3080 numbers came from the exp branch's `fd_timing` test, which exists in neither main nor rb. So all
three trees ran one identical harness, `pr-raw/4090/scripts/l3win.rs`, dropped into `common/examples/` on
the box:

- it reproduces `fd_timing`'s protocol: a window at the base's range start, one warm-up (JIT) call, then 3
  timed calls, with rate = window × reps / elapsed;
- it calls the production entry point `process_range_detailed_cubecl`, the call `nice_client` makes per
  field;
- it prints a digest of the results;
- it ran 3 rounds, interleaved main, rb, fd (`ab_final/win`).

| tree | b40 rounds | median | × main | b50 rounds | median | × main |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| main | 2.961 3.126 3.121 e10 | 3.121e10 | 1.000 | 2.287 2.300 2.301 e10 | 2.300e10 | 1.000 |
| rb | 3.192 3.191 3.189 e10 | 3.191e10 | **1.023** | 2.316 2.333 2.311 e10 | 2.316e10 | **1.007** |
| fd | 9.433 9.432 9.431 e10 | 9.432e10 | **3.022** | 8.356 8.358 8.351 e10 | 8.356e10 | **3.634** |

- **25 reps per cell** (`ab_final/win25`, same 3 rounds): fd/main **3.060× and 3.696×**; rb/main 1.021× and
  0.990×. rb's b50 is within noise; main's spread is 2.6 %.
- **The 3080's exact invocation.** I ran `fd_timing` itself on the exp branch `e0c1b4c`:

| R | b40 | b50 |

  - On the 4090, R = 256 is 0.3–0.4 % better than 128, within noise.

## 4. 1e10-number fields through the production path

This uses `pr-raw/4090/scripts/mock_api.py`, a local stand-in for `/claim/detailed` and `/submit`. It
serves the same 1e10 field at the base's range start on every claim and keeps every submission.

- `nice_client detailed --gpu --repeat` ran against it and was stopped by PID after 8 submissions
  (`prod1e10.sh`).
- That is claim → `process_field_sync` → `process_range_detailed_cubecl` → compile → submit, which is the
  real production path.
- It ran 3 rounds × main, rb, fd × b40 and b50 (`ab_final/prod`).
- Rates are the client's own `✓ Processed` line for fields 2–8. The first field includes kernel JIT and
  is listed separately.

| tree | b40 s/field | n/s | × main | b50 s/field | n/s | × main | first field (JIT) b40 / b50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| main | 0.328 | 3.05e10 | 1.000 | 0.457 | 2.19e10 | 1.000 | 0.40 / 0.52 s |
| rb | 0.329 | 3.04e10 | 0.997 | 0.459 | 2.18e10 | 0.995 | 0.40 / 0.52 s |
| fd | **0.107** | 9.34e10 | **3.061** | **0.121** | 8.25e10 | **3.767** | 0.29 / 0.34 s |

- **Spreads** are 2–5 % within each tree, with 21–24 fields each.
- **rb is a no-op at this size.** It saves one copy of about 1 ms per field, on a 330 ms field.
- **fd turns a 1e10 field into about 0.1 s of GPU.** A 4090 would want about 9 claims per second.
  - The client already prefetches claims and submits asynchronously.
  - But the API's claim rate and the per-field round trip, which was not measured here (local mock),
    become the question for production throughput.
- **NiceMark understates production.** Its 2e8 windows give 7 % (b40) and 10 % (b50) below this for fd,
  and 17 % and 14 % below for main. That is per-call host overhead on short windows.

## 6. Against the 3080 (GPU-FD.md)

The 3080 was capped at 180 W. There FD v3 against the division kernel was:

- 3.28× and 3.97× on 2e9 windows;
- 3.22× and 3.97× in the harness with both arms trimmed;
- 3.48× and 4.15× against today's full-read production path.

The readback fix alone was +8 % and +5 %.

On the uncapped but thermally limited 4090 the kernel ratio is about 9 % lower at both bases:

- `fd_timing` at 2e9, same invocation: 2.99× and 3.60×;
- 2e9 windows through the production call: fd/rb 2.96× and 3.61×;
- NiceMark windows: fd/rb 3.04× and 3.59×; fd/main **3.46× and 3.92×**.

**The gap is clock, not code.** Under the 3080's power cap FD gained 1.18× of clock over the IMAD-heavy
division kernel. Here, where heat is the limit, it gains only 1.03–1.09×. Per clock, FD against the
trimmed division kernel is 2.87× and 3.31× here, against 2.78× and 3.42× on the 3080. That matches within
about 3 %, so the per-clock gain belongs to the code, as the Ampere and Ada integer pipes would suggest.

**b50 still gains more than b40**, as predicted by limb count.

**The readback fix is worth more on the 4090** (+14 % and +9 % on NiceMark), despite PCIe 4. A 2e8
window takes only about 7–8 ms here, so the fixed 20 MB copy, about 1 ms, is a bigger share. On
production-size fields it is worth nothing on either card.

## 7. Not done or caveats

- **wgpu FD exactness** ran on llvmpipe only (section 5). The NVIDIA Vulkan path is unverified here.
- **The 1e10 production runs** use a local mock API, so they exclude the real claim/submit latency. I ran
  3 rounds × 8 fields rather than one field each.
- **The fixed windows** use my harness (identical across trees), not `fd_timing`, which main and rb lack.
  The `fd_timing` invocation was rerun on `e0c1b4c` for the like-for-like 3080 comparison.
- **Main's round-3 NiceMark dip** (section 2) is unexplained. My hypothesis is host-side copy contention.
  It does not move the medians.
- I did not use profiler counters or re-pin the NiceMark references.
